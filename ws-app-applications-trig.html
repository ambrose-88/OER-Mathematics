    Definition D.8.392. Bearings.

    The bearing from a point \(O\) to a point \(P\) is the acute angle, measured in degrees, between the ray \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) and the north-south line passing through \(O\text{.}\) The notation for bearings has three parts:
    • A letter \(N\) or \(S\) which determines if we are measuring from the north or south side of the north-south line.
      🔗
      🔗
    • The measure of the acute angle between the ray \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) and the north-south line.
      🔗
      🔗
    • A letter \(E\) or \(W\) indicating if the point \(P\) is east or west of the north-south line.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition D.8.397. Simple Harmonic Motion.

    A common application of trigonometric functions is to model the motion of an object that oscillates back and forth, such as a pendulum or a mass on a spring. This type of motion is called simple harmonic motion.
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem D.8.398. Modeling Simple Harmonic Motion.

    An object that moves on the coordinate-plane is in simple harmonic motion if its distance \(d\) from its equilibrium position at time \(t\) can be modeled by a function of the form
    \begin{equation*} d(t)=A\sin(\omega t) \end{equation*}
    or
    \begin{equation*} d(t)=A\cos(\omega t)\text{,} \end{equation*}
    where
    • the amplitude \(|A|\) measures the maximum displacement of the object from its equilibrium position, and
      🔗
      🔗
    • the period \(\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}\) measures the time it takes for the object to complete one full cycle of motion.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example D.8.399. Modeling Simple Harmonic Motion.

    A mass on a spring oscillates back and forth with simple harmonic motion. The mass is at its equilibrium position at time \(t=0\text{,}\) and the maximum distance from the equilibrium position is 5 inches. The period of the motion is 2 seconds.
    • Find a function that models the distance of the mass from its equilibrium position at time \(t\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • How far is the mass from its equilibrium position at time \(t=1.5\) seconds? Round your answer to two decimal places.
      🔗
      🔗

    Definition D.8.400. Frequency.

    The frequency of an object in simple harmonic motion is the number of cycles it completes in one unit of time.
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem D.8.401. Computing Frequency.

    If an object is in simple harmonic motion with period \(P\text{,}\) then the frequency of the motion is given by the formula
    \begin{equation*} f=\dfrac{1}{P}=\dfrac{\omega}{2\pi} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
