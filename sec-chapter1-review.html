    The wheels of a certain bicycle has a \(28\text{ in}\) diameter. If the wheels are rotating at a constant rate of \(150\text{ revolutions per minute}\text{,}\) how fast is the bicycle moving in miles per hour? Round your answer to \(2\) decimal places. Include the correct units in your answer.

    Hint.
    • Writing all the units out in your calculation will help with the unit conversions needed to solve this problem.
      🔗
      🔗
    • For example, write \(150\text{ revolutions per minute}\) as \(\dfrac{150\text{ revolutions}}{1\text{ minute}}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Your goal is to make the the units of measurement in the numerator miles and the denominator hours.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Remember, units cancel in the numerator and denominator just like numbers do!
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    The bicycle is moving at approximately \(12.49\text{ miles per hour}\text{.}\)

    Example 13.1.11. Find the Six Trigonometric Function using Right Trangles.

    Suppose \(\theta\) is one of the non-right angles in a right triangle where the side opposite \(\theta\) has length \(7\text {cm}\) and has a hypotenuse of length \(13\text{ cm}\text{.}\) Find the exact value of the six trigonometric functions of \(\theta\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{align*} \sin(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{7}{13}\amp \csc(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{13}{7}\\ \\ \cos(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{\sqrt{120}}{13}\amp \sec(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{13}{\sqrt{120}}\\ \\ \tan(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{7}{\sqrt{120}}\amp \cot(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{\sqrt{120}}{7} \end{align*}
    Note: \(\sqrt{120}=2\sqrt{30}\text{,}\) which means the following are also acceptable answers:
    \begin{align*} \cos(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{2\sqrt{30}}{13}\\ \\ \sec(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{13}{2\sqrt{30}}=\dfrac{13\sqrt{30}}{60}\\ \\ \tan(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{7}{2\sqrt{30}}=\dfrac{7\sqrt{30}}{60}\\ \\ \cot(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{2\sqrt{30}}{7} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.1.17. Using Reference Angles to Evaluate Trigonometric Functions.

    Suppose the point \((-120,209)\) is a point on the terminal side of an angle \(\theta\) in standard position. Draw the reference triangle for this point, label the sides and the reference angle, and find the exact value of the six trigonometric functions of \(\theta\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The six trigonometric functions of \(\theta\) are calculated below.
    \begin{align*} \sin(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{209}{241}\amp \csc(\theta)\amp=\dfrac{241}{209}\\ \\ \cos(\theta)\amp=-\dfrac{120}{241}\amp \sec(\theta)\amp=-\dfrac{241}{120}\\ \\ \tan(\theta)\amp=-\dfrac{209}{120}\amp \cot(\theta)\amp=-\dfrac{120}{209} \end{align*}
    🔗
