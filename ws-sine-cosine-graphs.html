    Worksheet D.5 Graphs of Sine and Cosine
    

    Definition D.5.265. Graph of the Sine Function.

    The equation \(y=\sin(x)\) is found by plotting the points \((x,y)=(\theta,\sin(\theta))\) where \(x\) is measured in radians.
    🔗
    Instead of a circle, this graph is a wave that oscillates between \(y=-1\) and \(y=+1\text{,}\) and this wave pattern repeats every \(2\pi\) units on the \(x\)-axis. Unless more of the graph is needed, we typically only draw one period of the graph.
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition D.5.268. Graph of the Cosine Function.

    The equation \(y=\cos(x)\) is found by plotting the points \((x,y)=(\theta,\cos(\theta))\) where \(x\) is measured in radians.
    🔗
    Similar to the sine graph, the graph of cosine is a wave that oscillates between \(y=-1\) and \(y=+1\text{,}\) and this wave pattern also repeats every \(2\pi\) units on the \(x\)-axis. Furthermore, if we consider the cofunction identity
    \begin{equation*} \cos(x) = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - x\right)\text{,} \end{equation*}
    we see that the graph of cosine is a transformation of the sine graph by performing a horizontal reflection and then a shift right by \(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition D.5.271. The Amplitude of Sine and Cosine Graphs.

    Let \(y=A\sin(x)\) or \(y=A\cos(x)\) where \(A\ne 0\text{.}\) The amplitude of the graph is one-half the vertical distance between the absolute maximum and the absolute minimum of the graph. The distance measured by the amplitude is given by the formula
    \begin{equation*} \text{amplitude} = |A|. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Note D.5.274. Algebraic Interpretation of Amplitude.

    The amplitude measure the vertical stretch or compression applied to the base graph \(y=\sin(x)\) or \(y=\cos(x)\text{.}\) In order to graph \(y=A\sin(x)\) or \(y=A\cos(x)\text{,}\) we can first graph the base graph and multiply the \(y\)-coordinates by \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Notice we didn’t apply absolute values to this multiplication. If \(A\) is negative, multiplying by \(A\) will also reflect the graph across the \(x\)-axis in addition to stretching or compressing the graph.
    🔗
    🔗

    Procedure D.5.275. Rapid Sketch Method (Amplitude Only Version).

    In order to rapidly sketch the graph of \(y=A\sin(x)\) or \(y=A\cos(x)\text{,}\) we only graph the absolute maximums, the absolute minimums, and the zeroes of the function. For both functions, these points correspond to the \(x\)-coordinates that are multiples of \(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    For \(y=A\sin(x)\text{,}\) the pattern of points for one period (starting at \(x=0\)) is:
    \begin{align*} (0,0)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2},A\right)\amp\amp(\pi,0)\amp\amp\left(\frac{3\pi}{2},-A\right)\amp\amp(2\pi,0) \end{align*}
    🔗
    For \(y=A\cos(x)\text{,}\) the pattern of points for one period (starting at \(x=0\)) is:
    \begin{align*} \left(0,A\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2},0\right)\amp\amp(\pi,-A)\amp\amp\left(\frac{3\pi}{2},0\right)\amp\amp(2\pi,A) \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition D.5.282.

    Let \(y=A\sin(Bx)\) or \(y=A\cos(Bx)\) where \(A\ne 0\) and \(B\gt 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The period of the graph is the horizontal distance between consecutive absolute maximums (or equivalently between consecutive absolute minimums) of the graph. The length of this horizontal distance is given by the following formula:
    \begin{equation*} \text{period} = \dfrac{2\pi}{B}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Procedure D.5.285. Rapid Sketch Method (Amplitude and Period Only Version).

    In order to rapidly sketch the graph of \(y=A\sin(Bx)\) or \(y=A\cos(Bx)\text{,}\) we only graph the absolute maximums, the absolute minimums, and the zeroes of the function. For both functions, these points correspond to the \(x\)-coordinates that are multiples of \(\dfrac{\pi}{2B}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    For \(y=A\sin(Bx)\text{,}\) the pattern of points for one period (starting at \(x=0\)) is:
    \begin{align*} (0,0)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2B},A\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{\pi}{B},0\right)\amp\amp\left(\frac{3\pi}{2B},-A\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{B},0\right) \end{align*}
    🔗
    For \(y=A\cos(Bx)\text{,}\) the pattern of points for one period (starting at \(x=0\)) is:
    \begin{align*} \left(0,A\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2B},0\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{\pi}{B},-A\right)\amp\amp\left(\frac{3\pi}{2B},0\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{B},A\right) \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example D.5.292. Finding the Equation from the Graph of a Sine or Cosine Function.

    Find the equation of the sine or cosine graph shown below.
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition D.5.294.

    Let \(y=A\sin(Bx-C)\) or \(y=A\cos(Bx-C)\) where \(A\ne 0\text{,}\) \(B\gt 0\text{,}\) and \(C\) is any real number.
    🔗
    The phase shift of the graph is the horizontal distance the graph of \(y=A\sin(Bx)\) or \(y=A\cos(Bx)\) is shifted left or right. The length of the shift is given by the following equation:
    \begin{equation*} \text{phase shift} = \frac{C}{B}. \end{equation*}
    If the phase shift is positive, the graph is shifted to the right, and if the phase shift is negative, the graph is shifted to the left.
    🔗
    🔗

    Procedure D.5.296. Rapid Sketch Method (Amplitude, Period, Phase Shift Only Version).

    In order to rapidly sketch the graph of \(y=A\sin(Bx-C)\) or \(y=A\cos(Bx-C)\text{,}\) we only graph the absolute maximums, the absolute minimums, and the zeroes of the function. For both functions, these points correspond to the \(x\)-coordinates that are equal to \(\dfrac{C}{B}\) plus a multiple of \(\dfrac{\pi}{2B}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    For \(y=A\sin(Bx-C)\text{,}\) the pattern of points for one period (starting at \(x=\dfrac{C}{B}\)) is:
    \begin{align*} \left(\dfrac{C}{B},0\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{\pi}{2B},A\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{\pi}{B},0\right)\\ \left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\frac{3\pi}{2B},-A\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{2\pi}{B},0\right) \end{align*}
    🔗
    For \(y=A\cos(Bx-C)\text{,}\) the pattern of points for one period (starting at \(x=\dfrac{C}{B}\)) is:
    \begin{align*} \left(\dfrac{C}{B},A\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{\pi}{2B},0\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{\pi}{B},-A\right)\\ \left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\frac{3\pi}{2B},0\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{2\pi}{B},A\right) \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition D.5.299.

    Let \(y=A\sin(Bx-C)+D\) or \(y=A\cos(Bx-C)+D\) where \(A\ne 0\text{,}\) \(B\gt 0\text{,}\) \(C\) is any real number, and \(D\) is any real number.
    🔗
    The vertical shift of the graph is the vertical distance the graph of \(y=A\sin(Bx-C)\) or \(y=A\cos(Bx-C)\) is shifted up or down. The length of the shift is given by the following equation:
    \begin{equation*} \text{vertical shift} = D. \end{equation*}
    If the vertical shift is positive, the graph is shifted up, and if the vertical shift is negative, the graph is shifted down.
    🔗
    🔗

    Procedure D.5.301. Rapid Sketch Method (Full Version).

    In order to rapidly sketch the graph of \(y=A\sin(Bx-C)+D\) or \(y=A\cos(Bx-C)+D\text{,}\) we only graph the absolute maximums, the absolute minimums, and the zeroes of the function. For both functions, these points correspond to the \(x\)-coordinates that are equal to \(\dfrac{C}{B}\) plus a multiple of \(\dfrac{\pi}{2B}\) and have \(D-A\text{,}\) \(D\text{,}\) or \(D+A\) as their \(y\)-coordinates.
    🔗
    For \(y=A\sin(Bx-C)+D\text{,}\) the pattern of points for one period (starting at \(x=\dfrac{C}{B}\)) is:
    \begin{align*} \left(\dfrac{C}{B},D\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{\pi}{2B},D+A\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{\pi}{B},D\right)\\ \left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\frac{3\pi}{2B},D-A\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{2\pi}{B},D\right) \end{align*}
    🔗
    For \(y=A\cos(Bx-C)+D\text{,}\) the pattern of points for one period (starting at \(x=\dfrac{C}{B}\)) is:
    \begin{align*} \left(\dfrac{C}{B},D+A\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{\pi}{2B},D\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{\pi}{B},D-A\right)\\ \left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\frac{3\pi}{2B},D\right)\amp\amp\left(\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{2\pi}{B},D+A\right) \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
